## BIDEA

Hallo. This is Liam, in for Bear. ReadySet asks a useful question: what changed in our data, and does that change matter for the analysis we want to run?

## BDEFS

Three terms. A release is one version of the data. A finding is a change supported by evidence. A review is a person deciding what to do with that evidence.

## B01

The useful idea is to connect three things: the data, the analysis, and the evidence for a decision. A change matters because of what it affects. That makes the question part of the checking process.

## B02

Here is the actual ReadySet interface, running locally with invented test data. A deliberately swapped measurement triggers a review. The headline tells us to pause, and the analysis cards tell us where to look.

## B03

Now the question changes. We leave out the affected protein analysis and keep the unrelated analysis. In this tested example, the verdict becomes Ready. That is a concrete demonstration of question-dependent checking.

## B04

Bring the affected analysis back, and the finding returns. This evidence view is a strength: the reader can inspect what changed, consider the suggested repair, and see the limits of the explanation.

## B05

The next strength is the handoff. Detection produces a finding; a person reviews it before deciding on action. The normal named-review path worked in our browser checks. That makes the decision inspectable.

## B06

Next steps can build on that foundation. Add an input-validity gate before comparing changes. If required evidence is missing, the process should stop there and explain what is needed to continue.

## B07

Then preserve each review as an event. A decision can change, but its earlier evidence and reasoning should remain inspectable. This is a practical extension of the evidence fingerprints the project already uses.

## B08

For the team, the opportunity is clear: keep the efficient detector and the readable evidence, then strengthen those boundaries. This is a promising research prototype. The demonstration does not establish clinical or production readiness.

## BVDT

Let's recap. Connect changes to the selected analysis. Let people inspect the evidence. Validate inputs before interpreting changes. Preserve the history of human decisions.
